Ongoing research

Historical redlining, urban heat & air pollution

My ongoing multi-city research examines how Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) neighborhood grades relate to current land surface temperature, air pollution, and demographic conditions in 24 U.S. cities.

The work compares urban contexts and the diurnal thermal cycle, with attention to satellite observation coverage, spatial sampling, and uncertainty.

Related peer-reviewed publication

Year-round heat exposure and social vulnerability

Using NASA ECOSTRESS land surface temperature observations and social vulnerability indicators, my published work assesses the spatial and seasonal distribution of thermal exposure in San Antonio.

The analysis connects thermal patterns, vegetation, and socioeconomic vulnerability to inform place-sensitive heat adaptation.

Active research

CFD, surrogate modeling & reinforcement learning

I develop and evaluate computational approaches for studying urban microclimates and potential mitigation strategies, including roof and surface interventions.

This research explores links among physically based simulation, faster surrogate prediction, and sequential decision making, while accounting for modeling assumptions and computational constraints.
